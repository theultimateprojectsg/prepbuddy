Unit 5: Soil

In this unit you will learn to:
- name the main parts of soil
- describe sandy soil, clay soil and loam
- choose which plants grow well in each kind of soil

Let's start
Mum's basil plant is dying. Its pot is full of water that will not drain away. Could the type of soil be the problem?

What is soil made of?
Soil is a mixture of:
- tiny pieces of rock (sand and clay), made when rocks break down over a long time
- humus: dead plants and animals that have rotted; it gives plants nutrients and makes soil dark
- water
- air (in the spaces between the pieces)
- living things such as earthworms, insects and bacteria
Try this: put some soil in a jar of water, shake it and leave it. The heavy pieces (stones, sand) settle at the bottom, then finer pieces (clay) on top, with humus floating on the water.

Three kinds of soil
1. Sandy soil
- Large pieces with big spaces between them.
- Feels rough and gritty.
- Water drains through it very quickly, so it is dry and holds few nutrients.
- Good for plants that need little water, such as cactus.
2. Clay soil
- Very tiny pieces packed closely together.
- Feels smooth and sticky when wet; hard and cracked when dry.
- Holds a lot of water and drains slowly, so it can become waterlogged. Little air.
- Good for plants that like plenty of water, such as lotus and rice.
3. Loam
- A mixture of sand, clay and lots of humus.
- Dark, crumbly and soft.
- Drains well but also holds enough water and air. Rich in nutrients.
- Best for most plants: vegetables, fruit trees and flowers.

A fair test: which soil drains fastest?
Put the same amount of each soil in three funnels with filter paper. Pour in the same amount of water. Measure how much water drips through in 5 minutes.
- Change: the type of soil.
- Measure: the volume of water that drains through.
- Keep the same: the amount of soil, the amount of water, the time, the funnels.
Usually sandy soil lets the most water through and clay lets the least.

Common mix-ups
- Thinking soil is just "dirt". It is full of living things and nutrients.
- Thinking clay soil is best because it holds the most water. Too much water and too little air harms most roots.

Challenge (for fast learners)
- How could a farmer make clay soil better for growing vegetables? (Add sand and compost (humus) so that it drains better and has more air.)

Key words
- soil: the top layer of the ground where plants grow (泥土)
- humus: rotted plant and animal material in soil (腐殖質)
- sandy soil: soil with large grains that drains quickly (沙質土)
- clay soil: soil with tiny grains that holds water (黏土)
- loam: a rich mixture of sand, clay and humus (壤土)
- drain: let water flow through and away (排水)
- nutrients: substances that living things need to grow (養分)
