Unit 9: How plants and animals adapt

In this unit you will learn to:
- give examples of plant features that help them live in their environment
- give examples of animal features that help them live in their environment
- describe animal behaviours that help them survive, such as migration and hibernation

Let's start
A cactus can live in a desert for weeks without rain. A polar bear can swim in icy water. How do they do it? Their bodies have special features that suit where they live. We say they are adapted to their environment.

Plant adaptations
- Cactus (desert): leaves are thin spines, so little water is lost and animals do not eat it; a thick, fleshy stem stores water; a waxy skin keeps water in; roots spread wide near the surface to catch rain quickly.
- Lotus (pond): big, flat leaves float on the water to get sunlight; the stems have air spaces, so air can reach the roots in the mud.
- Mangroves (muddy seashore, e.g. Mai Po): some roots stick up out of the mud to take in air; prop roots hold the tree firmly in soft mud.
- Pine trees (cold places): thin, needle-shaped leaves with a waxy coat lose little water and let snow slide off.
- Trees in rainforests: grow very tall to reach the sunlight; leaves have pointed "drip tips" so rain runs off quickly.

Animal adaptations (body features)
- Polar bear (Arctic): thick white fur and a layer of fat keep it warm; white colour helps it hide in the snow.
- Camel (desert): stores fat in its hump; long eyelashes keep out sand; wide feet stop it sinking in the sand.
- Fish (water): gills to breathe in water, fins to swim, a smooth streamlined body.
- Duck: webbed feet for swimming; oily feathers that keep out water.
- Chameleon and stick insect: colours or shapes that help them hide (camouflage).
- Eagle: sharp eyes to spot prey from high up; sharp claws and a hooked beak to catch and tear food.

Animal behaviours for survival
- Migration: moving to another place for part of the year, for food or warmth. Many birds fly south in winter. Black-faced spoonbills fly to Mai Po and Deep Bay in Hong Kong for the winter.
- Hibernation: a deep sleep through the winter, when food is hard to find. The body slows down to save energy. Some bears, frogs and snakes hibernate.
- Living in groups: zebras stay in herds for safety; bees work together in a hive.
- Hiding or playing dead to avoid being eaten.

Common mix-ups
- Thinking an animal can change its body because it "wants to". Adaptations are features that living things are born with, passed on from their parents over a very long time.
- Mixing up migration (moving away) and hibernation (sleeping through the winter).

Challenge (for fast learners)
- Design an imaginary animal that lives in a very cold, dark ocean. What features would help it survive? Explain each one.

Key words
- adapt / adaptation: a feature that helps a living thing survive in its environment (適應)
- environment: the surroundings where a living thing lives (環境)
- feature: a part of the body or a way it looks (特徵)
- camouflage: colours or shapes that help an animal hide (保護色)
- migration: moving to another place for part of the year (遷徙)
- hibernation: sleeping deeply through the winter (冬眠)
- survive: stay alive (生存)
