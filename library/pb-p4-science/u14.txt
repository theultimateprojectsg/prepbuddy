Unit 15: Electric circuits

In this unit you will learn to:
- name the parts of a simple circuit
- recognise a closed (complete) circuit
- explain why a circuit must be complete for an appliance to work
- use electricity safely

Let's start
Kevin connects a battery, two wires and a bulb, but the bulb does not light up. What has he done wrong? Let's find out.

Parts of a simple circuit
- Battery (cell): pushes electricity around the circuit. It has a positive end (+) and a negative end (−).
- Wires: carry electricity. They are made of metal (usually copper) with a plastic coating.
- Bulb: lights up when electricity flows through it. It has two contact points: the metal tip at the bottom and the metal side of its base.
- Switch: turns the circuit on and off.

Closed and open circuits
- Electricity can only flow when there is a complete loop with no gaps, from one end of the battery, through the wires and the bulb, and back to the other end of the battery. This is a closed circuit (complete circuit). The bulb lights up.
- If there is a gap anywhere, electricity cannot flow. This is an open circuit. The bulb does not light up.

Why might a bulb not light up?
- A wire is not touching one end of the battery.
- Both wires touch the same contact point of the bulb, or the same end of the battery.
- The switch is open (off).
- The bulb is broken or the battery is flat.

How a switch works
- When the switch is closed (on), it joins the gap, the circuit is complete and the bulb lights up.
- When the switch is open (off), there is a gap, so the electricity stops.
Every electrical appliance (a torch, a fan, a TV) needs a complete circuit to work. The switch makes or breaks the circuit.

Using electricity safely
- Batteries for experiments are safe, but the electricity from wall sockets is very strong and can kill.
- Never put anything into a socket.
- Never touch switches or plugs with wet hands.
- Do not plug too many things into one socket.
- Never play with damaged wires.

Common mix-ups
- Thinking one wire from the battery is enough. You need a full loop back to the battery.
- Thinking electricity is "used up" in the bulb. It flows all the way round the circuit.

Challenge (for fast learners)
- Use a battery, a bulb and wires to test which objects let electricity pass through: a coin, a rubber, a paper clip, a pencil lead, a plastic ruler. (See Unit 16.)
- Why is a torch switch put in the circuit, not just anywhere on the case?

Key words
- circuit: a path that electricity flows around (電路)
- closed circuit: a complete loop with no gaps (閉合電路)
- open circuit: a circuit with a gap (斷路)
- battery / cell: a source of electricity for a circuit (電池)
- bulb: a part that gives out light when electricity flows (燈泡)
- switch: a part that opens or closes a circuit (開關)
- wire: a thin metal thread that carries electricity (電線)
