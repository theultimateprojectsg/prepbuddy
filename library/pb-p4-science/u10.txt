Unit 11: How plants reproduce

In this unit you will learn to:
- describe how flowering plants make seeds (pollination, fertilisation, fruits and seeds)
- explain how seeds are spread
- know that some plants can grow new plants from roots, stems or leaves
- explain how plants increase their chances of having offspring

Let's start
Where do the seeds inside a watermelon come from? They started in the flower!

Parts of a flower (from P3)
- Petals: often brightly coloured to attract insects.
- Stamen: the male part. It makes pollen.
- Pistil: the female part. At the bottom is the ovary, which has ovules inside.

From flower to seed
1. Pollination: pollen is moved from a stamen to the top of a pistil.
   - By insects: bees and butterflies visit colourful, sweet-smelling flowers for nectar. Pollen sticks to them and is carried to another flower.
   - By wind: grass and corn flowers are small and dull, with lots of light pollen that blows in the wind.
2. Fertilisation: the pollen joins with an ovule inside the ovary.
3. The ovule grows into a seed. The ovary grows into a fruit that protects the seeds. The petals wither and fall off.

How seeds are spread (seed dispersal)
Seeds need to move away from the parent plant so that the young plants have enough light, water and space.
- By wind: light seeds with wings or fluffy hairs (dandelion, maple).
- By animals: animals eat juicy fruits (berries, tomatoes) and drop the seeds in their droppings; some seeds have hooks that catch on fur.
- By water: some fruits float, such as the coconut.
- By bursting: some pods dry and burst open, shooting out the seeds (peas, balsam).

New plants without seeds
Some plants can grow new plants from parts of the parent plant.
- Roots: sweet potato.
- Stems: potato (the potato we eat is a stem with "eyes" that grow shoots), ginger, strawberry runners, and cuttings from plants such as money plant.
- Leaves: the air plant (Bryophyllum, 落地生根) grows tiny plants along the edges of its leaves.
These new plants are just like the parent plant.

More offspring, better survival
Living things have different ways to make sure some young survive:
- Make many seeds or eggs: a tree makes thousands of seeds; a fish lays thousands of eggs. Only a few will survive.
- Protect the young: seeds have hard coats; birds' eggs have shells; many mammals and birds feed and protect their young.
- Spread out: seed dispersal helps young plants find space to grow.

Common mix-ups
- Thinking the fruit grows from the petals. It grows from the ovary.
- Thinking pollination and fertilisation are the same. Pollination moves the pollen; fertilisation is when the pollen joins the ovule.
- Thinking the potato we eat is a root. It is a stem.

Challenge (for fast learners)
- Why do plants pollinated by wind make far more pollen than plants pollinated by insects? (Most pollen blown by the wind never lands on the right flower.)

Try this at home
Put a piece of sweet potato or the top of a carrot in a shallow dish of water near a window. What grows after a week?

Key words
- reproduce: make new living things of the same kind (繁殖)
- pollen: tiny grains made by the stamen (花粉)
- pollination: moving pollen to the pistil (傳粉)
- fertilisation: when pollen joins an ovule (受精)
- ovary: the part of the flower that becomes the fruit (子房)
- seed dispersal: the spreading of seeds (種子傳播)
- offspring: young living things (後代)
