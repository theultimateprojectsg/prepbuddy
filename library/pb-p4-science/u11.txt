Unit 12: Inheritance: like parent, like child

In this unit you will learn to:
- know that young animals and plants look like their parents
- tell inherited characteristics from ones that are not inherited
- know that some human characteristics are inherited and cannot be changed by learning

Let's start
People often say, "You have your mother's eyes!" Kittens look like cats, and the seeds from a sunflower grow into sunflowers. Why?

Offspring are like their parents
- Living things make young of the same kind: dogs have puppies, mango seeds grow into mango trees.
- The young (offspring) get characteristics from their parents. This is called inheritance.
- Brothers and sisters often look alike, but not exactly the same, because each child gets a different mix of characteristics from both parents.

Inherited characteristics
These are passed from parents to children. You are born with them (or with the instructions for them). Examples in people:
- natural eye colour and hair colour
- whether your hair is naturally straight or curly
- earlobes that hang free or are attached to the side of the head
- being able to roll your tongue into a U-shape
- being able to bend your thumb backwards a lot
- dimples
You cannot learn to roll your tongue or bend your thumb back by practising if you were not born able to.

Characteristics that are NOT inherited
These come from what we do or what happens to us:
- the language you speak
- skills such as swimming, playing the piano or riding a bike
- a scar from a fall
- dyed hair or pierced ears
- big muscles from exercise
- a sun tan
A parent who learns to swim does not pass swimming on to their baby. The baby has to learn it.

Plants and animals too
- A white rabbit and a white rabbit usually have white babies.
- Seeds from a tall pea plant often grow into tall pea plants.
- But the environment matters too: a plant from a strong parent will still grow badly with no water or light.

Common mix-ups
- Thinking skills such as speaking English are inherited. They are learned.
- Thinking children look exactly like one parent. They get a mix from both.

Challenge (for fast learners)
- Make a class survey: how many classmates can roll their tongue? Show the results in a bar chart.
- Is height inherited? (Partly: tall parents often have tall children, but food, sleep and exercise also affect height.)

Key words
- inherit: get something from your parents (遺傳)
- inheritance: the passing of characteristics from parents to offspring (遺傳)
- characteristic: a feature of a living thing (特徵)
- offspring: young living things (後代)
- earlobe: the soft bottom part of the ear (耳垂)
- learned: got by practising, not born with (後天學習)
- similar: nearly the same (相似)
