Unit 3: Non-communicable diseases and healthy living

In this unit you will learn to:
- name some common non-communicable diseases
- describe their main causes and symptoms
- explain how a healthy lifestyle helps prevent them

Let's start
Grandpa has high blood pressure. Will you catch it if you sit next to him? No. Some diseases do not spread from person to person. They are non-communicable diseases.

What are non-communicable diseases?
They are not caused by germs, so they cannot be passed on by droplets, touch, food or mosquitoes. They usually develop slowly, over many years.

Some common non-communicable diseases
- Heart disease: the heart cannot pump blood well, often because the blood vessels are blocked by fat. Symptoms can include chest pain and getting out of breath easily.
- High blood pressure: blood pushes too hard on the walls of the blood vessels. There are often no symptoms, so it is important to check it.
- Diabetes: there is too much sugar in the blood. Symptoms include feeling very thirsty, going to the toilet often and feeling tired.
- Cancer: some cells in the body grow out of control and form a lump. Symptoms depend on where it is, for example a lump, losing weight without trying, or a cough that does not go away.

Main causes (risk factors)
- An unhealthy diet: too much fat, salt or sugar, and not enough fruit and vegetables.
- Not enough exercise.
- Being overweight.
- Smoking and breathing in second-hand smoke.
- Drinking too much alcohol.
- Some diseases also run in families (see Unit 12).

How to prevent them
- Eat a balanced diet: plenty of grains, fruit and vegetables; less fried food, salty snacks and sugary drinks.
- Exercise for at least 60 minutes a day (for children), for example running, swimming or cycling.
- Never smoke, and stay away from second-hand smoke.
- Sleep well and keep a healthy weight.
- Have regular health checks, so problems are found early.

Communicable or non-communicable?
- Flu, chickenpox, dengue fever, cholera → communicable (caused by germs, can spread).
- Heart disease, high blood pressure, diabetes, cancer → non-communicable (not caused by germs, cannot spread).

Common mix-ups
- Thinking only old people get these diseases. Unhealthy habits that start when you are young can cause them later.
- Thinking you can "catch" diabetes or cancer from someone. You cannot.

Challenge (for fast learners)
- Read the label on a can of soft drink. How many grams of sugar are in it? Why is this a problem if you drink one every day?

Try this at home
Keep a food diary for one day. Circle the foods that are high in fat, salt or sugar. What could you swap them for?

Key words
- non-communicable disease: a disease that cannot spread from person to person (非傳染病)
- heart disease: an illness of the heart and blood vessels (心臟病)
- diabetes: an illness with too much sugar in the blood (糖尿病)
- cancer: an illness where some cells grow out of control (癌症)
- risk factor: something that makes a disease more likely (風險因素)
- balanced diet: eating the right amounts of different foods (均衡飲食)
- second-hand smoke: smoke breathed in from other people's cigarettes (二手煙)
