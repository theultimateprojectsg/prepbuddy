Unit 10: Habitats and food chains

In this unit you will learn to:
- describe some natural environments and the living things found there
- explain the role of each living thing in a food chain
- identify predators and prey
- understand that living things compete for light, food and space

Let's start
In a pond, frogs eat insects, snakes eat frogs and herons eat snakes. If someone killed all the frogs, what would happen to the insects and the snakes?

Natural environments
- Tropical rainforest: hot and wet all year, with tall trees. Orangutans, toucans, tree frogs.
- Temperate grassland: wide open land covered with grass. Bison, prairie dogs.
- Savanna (tropical grassland): zebras, lions, giraffes, acacia trees.
- Desert: very dry. Camels, lizards, cactus.
- Polar regions: very cold with ice and snow. Polar bears in the Arctic; penguins in Antarctica.
- Hong Kong: woodlands, streams, rocky shores and wetlands such as Mai Po, with birds, crabs, mudskippers and mangroves.
Each living thing is suited to its own environment (see Unit 9).

Food chains
A food chain shows what eats what. The arrow means "is eaten by" (it shows the direction the food and energy go).
grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → eagle
- Producer: a green plant. It makes its own food using sunlight. Every food chain starts with a producer.
- Consumers: animals. They cannot make their own food, so they eat other living things.
  - Grasshopper: eats plants (a herbivore).
  - Frog, snake, eagle: eat other animals (carnivores).
- Most energy comes first from the Sun.

Predators and prey
- A predator hunts and eats other animals. A prey animal is hunted and eaten.
- In "grasshopper → frog", the frog is the predator and the grasshopper is its prey.
- The same animal can be both: a frog is a predator of grasshoppers and the prey of snakes.
- If the number of prey goes down, predators have less food, so their numbers go down too. If predators disappear, the prey can increase too much and eat too many plants.

Competition
Living things in the same place compete for things they need that are in short supply:
- Light: tall trees in a forest shade the plants below.
- Food: lions and hyenas both hunt zebras.
- Living space: barnacles crowd onto the same rock on a rocky shore.
- Water and minerals: weeds compete with vegetables in a garden.

Common mix-ups
- Drawing the arrows the wrong way. The arrow points TO the animal that does the eating: grass → rabbit.
- Starting a food chain with an animal. It always starts with a plant (a producer).
- Thinking predators are "bad". Every living thing plays a part in keeping nature balanced.

Challenge (for fast learners)
- In the chain leaf → caterpillar → bird → cat, what would happen to the number of leaves if all the birds were caught? (Caterpillars would increase and eat more leaves, so the leaves would decrease.)

Key words
- habitat: the natural home of a living thing (生境)
- food chain: a chain showing what eats what (食物鏈)
- producer: a green plant that makes its own food (生產者)
- consumer: an animal that eats other living things (消費者)
- predator: an animal that hunts other animals (捕食者)
- prey: an animal that is hunted (獵物)
- compete: try to get the same thing as others (競爭)
