Unit 7: Day and night, and the seasons

In this unit you will learn to:
- explain why we have day and night
- know that the Earth's axis is tilted
- explain why the seasons in the northern and southern hemispheres are opposite

Let's start
When it is morning in Hong Kong, it is evening of the day before in New York. And when Hong Kong is hot in July, it is winter in Australia. Why?

The Earth spins: day and night
- The Earth spins around an imaginary line called its axis. This spinning is called rotation.
- One full turn takes about 24 hours (one day).
- The Earth spins from west to east.
- The Sun can only light up half of the Earth at a time. The half facing the Sun has day. The half facing away has night.
- As the Earth turns, places move from night into day (sunrise) and from day into night (sunset).
- Because the Earth spins from west to east, the Sun seems to rise in the east and set in the west. The Sun is not really moving across the sky: we are turning.

Try this: shine a torch on a globe in a dark room. Turn the globe slowly from west to east. Watch Hong Kong move from day to night.

The Earth travels around the Sun: a year
- The Earth also moves around the Sun in a path called an orbit. This is called revolution.
- One full trip takes about 365 days (one year).

The tilted axis
The Earth's axis is not straight up and down. It is tilted (leaning to one side), and it always leans the same way as the Earth goes around the Sun.
- The equator is an imaginary line around the middle of the Earth.
- North of the equator is the northern hemisphere (Hong Kong, China, Japan, the UK).
- South of the equator is the southern hemisphere (Australia, New Zealand, South Africa).

Why we have seasons
Because of the tilt, each hemisphere leans towards the Sun for part of the year and away from it for another part.
- When the northern hemisphere leans towards the Sun (around June and July), the Sun is higher in the sky there. Sunlight is stronger and days are longer: it is summer in Hong Kong. At the same time, the southern hemisphere leans away: it is winter in Australia.
- Around December and January, it is the opposite: winter in Hong Kong and summer in Australia. Many people in Australia have a hot, sunny Christmas at the beach!
- Seasons are NOT caused by the Earth being closer to or further from the Sun.

Common mix-ups
- Thinking day and night happen because the Sun goes around the Earth. The Earth spins.
- Mixing up rotation (spinning, 1 day) and revolution (going around the Sun, 1 year).
- Thinking summer happens when the Earth is closest to the Sun. If that were true, both hemispheres would have summer at the same time.

Challenge (for fast learners)
- If the Earth's axis were not tilted, would we still have seasons? (No. Each place would get about the same sunlight all year.)
- Why do places near the North Pole have days in summer when the Sun never sets?

Key words
- rotation: the Earth spinning on its axis (自轉)
- revolution: the Earth moving around the Sun (公轉)
- axis: an imaginary line through the North and South Poles (地軸)
- tilted: leaning to one side (傾斜)
- hemisphere: half of the Earth, north or south of the equator (半球)
- equator: an imaginary line around the middle of the Earth (赤道)
- orbit: the path of one object around another (軌道)
