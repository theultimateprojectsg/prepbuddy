Unit 13: Friction

In this unit you will learn to:
- explain what friction is
- know that friction acts in the opposite direction to movement
- give everyday examples where friction helps us
- describe ways to increase or reduce friction

Let's start
Why is it easy to slip on a wet floor but hard to slip on a rough rubber mat? Why does a ball rolling on grass slow down and stop? The answer is friction.

What is friction?
- Friction is a force that tries to stop things from sliding or rubbing against each other.
- It happens when two surfaces rub together.
- Friction always acts in the opposite direction to the movement. If you push a box to the right, friction on the box acts to the left.
- Friction slows moving things down and can make them stop. It also produces heat: rub your hands together quickly and they feel warm.

What affects friction?
- Rough surfaces (sandpaper, carpet, rubber) make more friction.
- Smooth surfaces (ice, glass, polished floors) make less friction.
- Water, oil and soap between the surfaces reduce friction.
- Pressing the surfaces together harder makes more friction.

Friction helps us
- Walking: friction between our shoes and the ground stops us from slipping. Shoe soles have patterns to grip.
- Writing: friction between the pencil and paper leaves a mark. You cannot write well on glass.
- Holding things: friction stops a cup from slipping out of your hand.
- Stopping: bicycle brakes press rubber pads against the wheel. Car tyres have a pattern (tread) to grip the road.
- Lighting a match: rubbing the match on the rough side of the box.

Sometimes we want less friction
- Wheels roll more easily than things slide.
- Oil is put on bicycle chains and door hinges.
- Slides in the playground are smooth.
- Swimmers wear smooth swimsuits, and ice skates have smooth, thin blades.
- Moving parts in machines are oiled so they do not wear out and get hot.

A fair test: on which surface does a toy car travel furthest?
Let a toy car roll down the same ramp from the same height onto different surfaces (carpet, wood, tiles). Measure how far it travels.
- Change: the surface.
- Measure: the distance travelled (cm).
- Keep the same: the car, the ramp, the starting height, letting go without pushing.
The smoothest surface has the least friction, so the car goes furthest.

Common mix-ups
- Thinking friction is always bad. We could not walk, write or stop without it.
- Thinking friction acts in the same direction as the movement. It acts in the opposite direction.

Challenge (for fast learners)
- Why do roads become dangerous on rainy days? (The water reduces friction between tyres and road, so cars take longer to stop.)

Key words
- friction: a force that tries to stop surfaces sliding over each other (摩擦力)
- force: a push or a pull (力)
- surface: the outside or top layer of something (表面)
- rough: not smooth, bumpy (粗糙)
- smooth: flat and even, not rough (光滑)
- grip: hold tightly without slipping (抓緊)
- opposite direction: the other way (相反方向)
