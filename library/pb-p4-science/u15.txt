Unit 16: Metals, conductors and insulators

In this unit you will learn to:
- compare how well metals and non-metals let electricity and heat pass through
- link the properties of metals to their uses
- choose materials that are good for letting heat through or for keeping heat in or out

Let's start
Why does a metal spoon in hot soup get hot very quickly, but a wooden spoon does not? Why are electric wires made of copper but covered with plastic?

Conductors and insulators
- A conductor lets electricity or heat pass through easily.
- An insulator does not let electricity or heat pass through easily.

Electricity
- Metals (copper, iron, aluminium, steel) are good conductors of electricity.
- Non-metals such as plastic, rubber, wood, glass and paper are insulators. (Pencil lead, made of graphite, is an unusual non-metal that does conduct electricity.)
- Test it: put the object into a gap in a circuit. If the bulb lights up, it is a conductor.

Heat
- Metals are also good conductors of heat. A metal pan heats up quickly, so food cooks fast.
- Plastic, wood, rubber, cloth, wool, foam and air are poor conductors of heat: they are heat insulators.

Properties of metals and their uses
Metals are usually:
- good conductors of electricity → copper is used inside electric wires.
- good conductors of heat → aluminium and steel are used for pots, pans and kettles.
- strong and hard → steel is used for bridges, buildings and cars.
- shiny → silver and gold are used for jewellery.
- able to be bent or hammered into shapes without breaking → aluminium foil, cans.

Using conductors and insulators together
- An electric wire: copper inside (conducts electricity), plastic outside (insulates, so we do not get a shock).
- A frying pan: a metal base (conducts heat to the food), a plastic or wooden handle (insulates, so we do not burn our hands).
- A plug: metal pins, plastic case.

Keeping heat in or out (thermal insulation)
- A vacuum flask keeps hot drinks hot and cold drinks cold.
- A foam lunch box or cool box keeps ice from melting quickly.
- Woollen clothes trap air and keep us warm in winter.
- Oven gloves are thick and padded so we can hold hot trays.
- Houses in cold places use thick walls and double windows.
Insulators slow down heat moving in BOTH directions: they keep hot things hot and cold things cold.

Common mix-ups
- Thinking a woollen jumper "makes" heat. It only traps your body's heat.
- Thinking all shiny things are metal. Some plastics are shiny too; test them with a circuit.
- Thinking insulators only keep things warm. A cool box uses insulation to keep things cold.

Challenge (for fast learners)
- Plan a fair test to find which material keeps a cup of hot water warm the longest: foil, wool or bubble wrap. What will you change, measure and keep the same?

Key words
- conductor: a material that lets electricity or heat pass through easily (導體)
- insulator: a material that does not let electricity or heat pass through easily (絕緣體)
- conduct: let something pass through (傳導)
- metal: a material like iron or copper, usually shiny and hard (金屬)
- non-metal: a material that is not a metal, such as plastic or wood (非金屬)
- thermal: to do with heat (熱)
- property: what a material is like (特性)
