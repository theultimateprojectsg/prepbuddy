Unit 18: Space technology in daily life

In this unit you will learn to:
- explain how artificial satellites are used in daily life
- give examples of everyday products that came from space technology
- know some of our nation's astronauts and their achievements
- describe what life is like for astronauts in space

Let's start
How does your parents' phone map know where you are? How does the Hong Kong Observatory know a typhoon is coming? Both use satellites high above the Earth.

Artificial satellites
A satellite is an object that moves around a planet. The Moon is a natural satellite of the Earth. People have launched thousands of artificial satellites into orbit.
How satellites help us:
- Weather: weather satellites take pictures of clouds and typhoons from space, so forecasters can warn us early. China's Fengyun (風雲) satellites are weather satellites.
- Navigation: navigation satellites tell phones and cars exactly where they are. China's BeiDou (北斗) system and the GPS system are examples.
- Communication: satellites carry TV programmes, phone calls and internet signals around the world.
- Watching the Earth: satellites check forests, crops, floods and pollution.
Satellite impact: they help keep people safe and make daily life easier, but old satellites and broken pieces become space junk that could crash into other satellites.

Space technology in everyday products
Many things invented for space travel are now used at home:
- memory foam pillows and mattresses (first made to protect seats in aircraft and spacecraft)
- freeze-dried food, like instant noodle vegetables and astronaut ice cream
- ear thermometers that measure temperature with infrared light
- the tiny camera sensors in mobile phones
- cordless (battery-powered) tools
- water filters

Our nation's astronauts
- In 2003, Yang Liwei (楊利偉) became China's first astronaut in space, on the Shenzhou-5 spacecraft.
- In 2008, Zhai Zhigang (翟志剛) became the first Chinese astronaut to walk in space.
- Wang Yaping (王亞平) gave science lessons from space to students in China, including the "Tiangong Classroom" lessons from the space station.
- China built its own space station, Tiangong (天宮), where astronauts live and work for months at a time.
- China's Chang'e (嫦娥) missions brought rock samples back from the Moon.
These achievements took the work of many scientists and engineers over many years, and help the whole world learn more about space.

Life in space
In the space station, astronauts float because they are falling around the Earth together with the station.
- Sleeping: they sleep in sleeping bags fixed to the wall, so they do not float around.
- Eating: food comes in packets; drinks are sucked through straws, because liquids float away as balls.
- Exercise: muscles and bones get weaker in space, so astronauts exercise for about two hours every day on special machines.
- Water: water is carefully cleaned and reused, because every drop must be carried from Earth.
- Work: they do science experiments, check equipment and sometimes go on spacewalks in spacesuits.

Common mix-ups
- Thinking satellites are only for TV. They are used for weather, maps, communication and much more.
- Thinking there is no gravity at all in the space station. Astronauts float because they are in free fall around the Earth.

Challenge (for fast learners)
- Imagine you are designing a cup for astronauts. What problems must it solve? (Liquids float, so it must keep drinks inside and let astronauts sip them.)

Key words
- satellite: an object that moves around a planet (衞星)
- artificial: made by people (人造)
- orbit: the path of an object moving around a planet (軌道)
- navigation: finding the way (導航)
- astronaut: a person trained to travel in space (太空人)
- space station: a large spacecraft where astronauts live and work (太空站)
- spacewalk: when an astronaut goes outside a spacecraft (太空漫步)
