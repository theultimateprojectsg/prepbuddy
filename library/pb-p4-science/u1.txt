Unit 2: Communicable diseases

In this unit you will learn to:
- name some common communicable diseases, their causes and symptoms
- explain how they spread
- describe ways to stop them spreading
- see how science helps us fight big outbreaks

Let's start
Ming sneezes in class without covering his mouth. A few days later, three of his classmates have a cold too. Why? Because a cold is a communicable disease: it can pass from one person to another.

What causes communicable diseases?
They are caused by germs: tiny living things such as viruses and bacteria. They are too small to see without a microscope.
- Influenza (flu): caused by a virus. Symptoms: fever, cough, sore throat, aching body, tiredness.
- Common cold: caused by viruses. Symptoms: runny nose, sneezing, sore throat.
- Cholera: caused by bacteria in dirty water or food. Symptoms: very bad diarrhoea, which can make the body lose too much water.
- Dengue fever: caused by a virus carried by mosquitoes. Symptoms: high fever, headache, muscle and joint pain, rash.
- Chickenpox: caused by a virus. Symptoms: fever and itchy spots.

How do they spread?
- Droplets: tiny drops from sneezing, coughing or talking (flu, cold, chickenpox).
- Contact: touching an ill person, or touching things they touched, then touching your eyes, nose or mouth (hand, foot and mouth disease).
- Food and water: eating or drinking things with germs in them (cholera, food poisoning).
- Vectors: animals such as mosquitoes that carry germs from one person to another (dengue fever, malaria).
- Blood: through blood, for example sharing a needle or a razor (hepatitis B).

How to prevent them
- Wash your hands with liquid soap for at least 20 seconds, especially before eating and after using the toilet.
- Cover your mouth and nose with a tissue when you sneeze or cough. Wear a mask if you are ill.
- Eat food that is fully cooked. Drink boiled water.
- Do not share towels, toothbrushes or razors.
- Stop mosquitoes from breeding: empty water from flower pots and cans, and use insect repellent.
- Get vaccinated (for example, flu vaccine every year).
- If you are ill, rest at home and see a doctor.

Science helps us fight disease
Scientists make vaccines that teach the body to fight a germ. They also develop medicines and tests to find out quickly who is ill. During an outbreak, washing hands, wearing masks and testing help stop the disease from spreading to many people. Keeping germs and dangerous diseases under control protects everyone. This is called biosecurity.

Common mix-ups
- Thinking all diseases can spread from person to person. Heart disease and cancer cannot (see Unit 3).
- Thinking only people spread diseases. Mosquitoes and dirty food and water can too.
- Washing hands with water only, for a few seconds. Use soap and wash for at least 20 seconds.

Challenge (for fast learners)
- Why should we not take antibiotics for a cold? (Antibiotics kill bacteria. A cold is caused by viruses.)
- Why does the government ask people to clear standing water every week in summer?

Key words
- communicable disease: a disease that can spread from one living thing to another (傳染病)
- germ: a tiny living thing that can cause disease (病菌)
- virus: a very tiny germ, such as the flu virus (病毒)
- symptom: a sign that you are ill, such as a fever (病徵)
- droplet: a tiny drop from a sneeze or cough (飛沫)
- vector: an animal that carries germs, such as a mosquito (病媒)
- vaccine: medicine that helps the body fight a disease (疫苗)
