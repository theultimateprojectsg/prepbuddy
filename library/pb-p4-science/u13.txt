Unit 14: Light, reflection and shadows

In this unit you will learn to:
- tell light sources from objects that reflect light
- give examples of reflection
- explain how shadows form
- describe how shadows change during the day

Let's start
At night, the Moon shines brightly in the sky. Is the Moon a light source like the Sun? No! The Moon has no light of its own. It reflects light from the Sun.

Light sources
A light source gives out its own light.
- Natural: the Sun, stars, fire, lightning, fireflies.
- Man-made: torches, light bulbs, candles, screens.
We see a light source when its light goes straight into our eyes. Never look directly at the Sun: it can damage your eyes.

Reflected light
Most things do not give out light. We see them because light from a light source bounces off them into our eyes. This bouncing is called reflection.
- The Moon reflects sunlight.
- A mirror reflects light very well, so we can see a clear image.
- A still pond or a calm sea reflects the sky and trees, like a mirror.
- Shiny surfaces (metal spoons, glass windows) reflect more light than dull ones.
- In the dark, we cannot see anything, because there is no light to be reflected.

Light travels in straight lines
You can see this when sunlight comes through a gap in the clouds or when a torch beam shines through dust.

Shadows
- A shadow forms when an object blocks light. Light cannot bend around the object, so there is a dark area behind it.
- Objects that light cannot pass through (wood, metal, our bodies) are opaque. They make dark shadows.
- A shadow is always on the opposite side of the object from the light source.
- The shape of the shadow is like the outline of the object.

Shadows in sunlight change during the day
The Sun seems to move across the sky from east to west.
- Morning: the Sun is low in the east. Shadows are long and point towards the west.
- Around noon: the Sun is high in the sky. Shadows are shortest.
- Afternoon and evening: the Sun is low in the west. Shadows are long and point towards the east.
The lower the Sun (or any light source), the longer the shadow. The higher the light, the shorter the shadow.
Long ago, people used sundials: the position of a stick's shadow told them the time.

Common mix-ups
- Thinking the Moon is a light source.
- Thinking a shadow points towards the Sun. It points away from the Sun.
- Thinking a mirror is a light source. It only reflects light.

Challenge (for fast learners)
- At 4 p.m. your shadow points east. In which direction is the Sun? (West, because the shadow is on the opposite side.)
- Why are shadows longer in winter in Hong Kong than at the same time of day in summer? (The Sun is lower in the sky in winter.)

Try this at home
On a sunny day, stand a pencil upright in some modelling clay on a piece of paper by a window. Mark the end of its shadow every hour. What do you notice?

Key words
- light source: something that gives out its own light (光源)
- reflect / reflection: bounce light off a surface (反射)
- mirror: a shiny surface that reflects a clear image (鏡)
- shadow: a dark area made when an object blocks light (影子)
- opaque: does not let light pass through (不透光)
- block: stop something from passing (阻擋)
- sundial: a clock that uses a shadow to show the time (日晷)
