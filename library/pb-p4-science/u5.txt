Unit 6: Sources of energy and saving energy

In this unit you will learn to:
- name the main sources of energy
- tell renewable sources from non-renewable sources
- explain why we need energy every day
- give ways to save energy

Let's start
Switch on a light, ride the MTR, charge a tablet, cook dinner: all of these need energy. Where does that energy come from?

Sources of energy
- The Sun: gives light and heat. Solar panels turn sunlight into electricity.
- Wind: moving air turns wind turbines to make electricity. Hong Kong has a wind turbine on Lamma Island.
- Moving water: flowing rivers and falling water turn turbines in hydroelectric power stations.
- Coal, crude oil and natural gas: these are fossil fuels. They formed from plants and animals that died millions of years ago. Burning them gives heat, which is used to make electricity and to run vehicles. Much of Hong Kong's electricity comes from burning natural gas and coal.
- Food is the energy source for our bodies.

Renewable and non-renewable
- Renewable sources will not run out: sunlight, wind, moving water.
- Non-renewable sources will run out one day, because they take millions of years to form: coal, crude oil, natural gas.
Burning fossil fuels also produces smoke and carbon dioxide, which pollute the air and add to global warming (see Unit 8).

What do we use energy for?
- Transport: cars, buses, trains, planes and ships (petrol, diesel, electricity).
- Making things in factories (manufacturing).
- Lighting our homes, streets and buildings.
- Running electronic devices: phones, computers, TVs, air conditioners.
- Heating and cooking.

Why save energy?
- Fossil fuels will run out.
- Saving energy means less pollution and less global warming.
- It saves money on electricity bills.

How to save energy
- Switch off lights, fans and computers when you leave a room.
- Set the air conditioner to 25.5°C or higher, and close doors and windows when it is on.
- Unplug chargers when they are not in use.
- Use LED light bulbs and appliances with a good energy label.
- Walk or take public transport instead of a car.
- Use the stairs for one or two floors instead of the lift.

Common mix-ups
- Thinking electricity is a source of energy. Electricity is made FROM a source, such as coal, gas, wind or sunlight.
- Thinking renewable means "free and unlimited at all times". The Sun does not shine at night and the wind does not always blow.

Challenge (for fast learners)
- Why does Hong Kong not use more hydroelectric power? (It has no big rivers or high waterfalls.)

Try this at home
Walk around your home and count how many things are plugged in but switched off at the wall. Which ones could be unplugged?

Key words
- energy: what is needed to make things work, move or heat up (能量)
- source: where something comes from (來源)
- fossil fuel: coal, oil or gas formed from living things long ago (化石燃料)
- renewable: will not run out (可再生)
- non-renewable: will run out one day (不可再生)
- solar panel: a panel that turns sunlight into electricity (太陽能板)
- save energy: use less energy (節約能源)
