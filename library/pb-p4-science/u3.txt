Unit 4: Inside the Earth and its rocks

In this unit you will learn to:
- describe the three main layers of the Earth
- say what each layer is like
- name the three kinds of rock in the Earth's crust and how they form

Let's start
If you could dig a hole straight down from Hong Kong, what would you find? First rock and soil, then very hot rock, and finally a hot metal centre. You could never really dig that far: the centre is over 6000 km away!

The three layers of the Earth
Think of a peach: the thin skin, the soft flesh and the hard stone in the middle.
- The crust: the outer layer we live on. It is made of solid rock. It is the thinnest layer: thinner under the oceans and thicker under the continents.
- The mantle: the thick layer under the crust. It is made of very hot rock. Some of it is soft and moves very slowly, like thick toothpaste. It is the thickest layer.
- The core: the centre of the Earth. It is made mostly of the metals iron and nickel. It is the hottest part. The outer core is liquid and the inner core is solid, because it is pressed so hard.
The deeper you go, the hotter it gets.

Rocks in the crust
The crust is made of three kinds of rock.
1. Igneous rock
- Forms when hot melted rock (magma) from deep underground cools and becomes solid.
- Examples: granite (often with shiny crystals; used for buildings, steps and kerbs), basalt.
- In Hong Kong, the six-sided rock columns at High Island in Sai Kung are volcanic rock.
2. Sedimentary rock
- Forms when small pieces of sand, mud and shells settle in layers, usually under water, and are pressed together over a very long time.
- Examples: sandstone, shale, limestone.
- You can often see layers in it, and sometimes fossils.
3. Metamorphic rock
- Forms when igneous or sedimentary rock is changed by great heat and pressure deep underground.
- Examples: marble (from limestone), slate (from shale).
- Marble is used for floors and statues; slate is used for roof tiles.

How rocks are useful
Building (granite, marble), roofs (slate), making cement (limestone).

Common mix-ups
- Thinking the mantle is all liquid. Most of it is solid but soft enough to move very slowly.
- Thinking the crust is the thickest layer. It is the thinnest.
- Mixing up the rocks: "igneous" comes from melted rock; "sedimentary" from layers; "metamorphic" means "changed".

Challenge (for fast learners)
- A piece of rock has clear layers and a shell fossil in it. What kind of rock is it likely to be, and why? (Sedimentary: it formed in layers under water, where shells could be buried.)

Try this at home
Look at the floors, walls and steps in your building or at a shopping centre. Can you spot granite or marble?

Key words
- crust: the thin outer layer of the Earth (地殼)
- mantle: the thick hot layer under the crust (地幔)
- core: the hot metal centre of the Earth (地核)
- igneous rock: rock formed when melted rock cools (火成岩)
- sedimentary rock: rock formed from layers pressed together (沉積岩)
- metamorphic rock: rock changed by heat and pressure (變質岩)
- magma: melted rock under the ground (岩漿)
