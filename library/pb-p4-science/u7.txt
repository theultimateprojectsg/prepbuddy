Unit 8: Weather, climate and global warming

In this unit you will learn to:
- tell the difference between weather and climate
- describe the weather in different places
- explain what global warming is and how it affects the Earth
- suggest ways to slow down global warming

Let's start
"It's raining today, so take an umbrella." "Hong Kong has hot, wet summers." Which sentence is about weather and which is about climate?

Weather and climate
- Weather is what the air outside is like at a certain time and place: hot or cold, sunny, cloudy, rainy, windy, humid. It can change from hour to hour and day to day.
- Climate is the usual pattern of weather in a place over many years (usually 30 years or more).
- "It's raining today" → weather. "Hong Kong has hot, wet summers" → climate.

Weather features
- Temperature: how hot or cold it is (in °C). It is usually coolest just before sunrise and warmest in the early afternoon.
- Rainfall: how much rain falls (in mm).
- Snowfall: snow falls when it is very cold (below 0°C).
- Humidity: how much water vapour is in the air. On a humid day your skin feels sticky.
- Wind: how fast and from which direction the air moves.

Weather in different places
- Hong Kong: subtropical. Summers are hot, humid and rainy, with typhoons; winters are cool and dry.
- Tropical rainforests (e.g. the Amazon): hot and wet all year.
- Deserts (e.g. the Sahara): very little rain; very hot in the day and cold at night.
- Polar regions (the Arctic, Antarctica): very cold all year, with snow and ice.
- Beijing: hot summers and cold winters, sometimes with snow.

Global warming
- The Earth's average temperature is slowly rising. This is called global warming.
- The main cause: burning fossil fuels (coal, oil, gas) releases carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases. These gases trap heat around the Earth, like a blanket. Cutting down forests also adds to it, because trees take in carbon dioxide.
Impacts of global warming:
- Glaciers and polar ice melt.
- Sea levels rise, so low coastal areas may flood.
- Some dry areas turn into desert (desertification).
- More extreme weather: stronger typhoons, heavier rain, more very hot days.
- Some animals and plants lose their homes, for example polar bears lose the sea ice they hunt on.

How we can slow down global warming
- Save electricity (see Unit 6).
- Use renewable energy such as solar and wind.
- Walk, cycle or use public transport.
- Plant trees and protect forests.
- Reduce, reuse and recycle, so less energy is used to make new things.
- Eat less wasted food.

Common mix-ups
- Using "weather" and "climate" as if they mean the same thing.
- Thinking one cold winter means global warming has stopped. Global warming is about the average over many years.

Challenge (for fast learners)
- The Hong Kong Observatory says the number of very hot days in Hong Kong has gone up over the past 100 years. Is this about weather or climate? (Climate: it is a pattern over many years.)

Try this at home
Record the temperature and the weather at the same time every day for a week. Draw a bar chart of the temperatures.

Key words
- weather: what the air is like at a certain time and place (天氣)
- climate: the usual weather pattern in a place over many years (氣候)
- temperature: how hot or cold something is (溫度)
- humidity: how much water vapour is in the air (濕度)
- global warming: the slow rise in the Earth's average temperature (全球暖化)
- greenhouse gas: a gas that traps heat, such as carbon dioxide (溫室氣體)
- sea level: the height of the sea's surface (海平面)
