Equity Linked Note (ELN)
Generic Product Description
An Equity Linked Note (ELN) is a
structured derivative, the return on
which depends on the performance of
one or more underlying equity shares
or indices. An ELN may pay a higher
yield than traditional money market
investments reflecting the risk of the
underlying share or index.
An ELN with an underlying share (or
reference share) can be structured to
allow for physical settlement or cash
settlement on the redemption date.
When the underlying is an index,
physical settlement is not possible
and the ELN will be settled in cash on
the redemption date.
An ELN can be tailored to suit
different risk/return profiles.
How Does an ELN work?
You purchase an ELN at a discount to the
principal amount of the ELN after agreeing on
the tenor, currency, underlying stock or index,
strike level and yield.
The repayment of the principal amount on the
redemption date is dependent on the closing
price of the underlying as observed on the final
fixing date, which is usually 2 or 3 business days
before redemption date. If the closing price of
the underlying is at or above the strike level, the
ELN will be redeemed at 100% of its principal
amount in cash. The gain will be the difference
between the discounted purchase price and the
principal amount of the ELN.
For an ELN with an underlying share, if the
closing price of the underlying on the final
fixing date is below the strike level, you will
receive physical shares or their cash equivalent.
For every ELN you hold, the number of shares
you receive will be calculated by dividing the
denomination of the ELN by the strike level. The
cash equivalent represents the market closing
value of that number of shares on the final
fixing date. This will result in a loss to you.
Where the ELN is physically settled, the loss will
not be realized until you sell any shares you
receive.
For an ELN with an underlying index, if the
closing price of the index is below the strike
level, you will receive on the redemption date
an amount in cash for every ELN held. The cash
amount is calculated by multiplying the
denomination of the ELN by the closing price of
the index and then dividing by the strike level.
This will result in a loss to you.

What are the benefits of an ELN?
− An ELN may provide a higher yield on the
redemption date compared with traditional
money market investments
− An ELN is likely to outperform a direct
investment in the underlying share or index
if the price of the underlying share or index
remains flat or only slightly positive during
the tenor of the ELN
− The downside risk of investing in an ELN
would be no higher than the downside risk
of a direct investment in the underlying
share or index
What are the key risks of an ELN?
− An ELN is not a principal protected
investment. If the closing price of the
underlying share or index is below the strike
level on the final fixing date, you are
exposed on the downside to the market
value of the underlying share or index. The
underlying share or index can trade
considerably below the strike level, which
may lead to a partial or total loss of the
principal amount
− An ELN’s potential return is limited on the
upside to the difference between the
discounted purchase price and the principal
amount of the ELN. If the price of the
underlying share or the index rises
considerably, you may have obtained a
better return by a direct purchase of the
underlying share or index.
− An ELN may have little or no liquidity. You
should be prepared to hold the ELN for its
entire tenor. Furthermore, if a secondary
market exists, the value of the ELN will
fluctuate depending on a number of factors
such as the movement in the underlying
share price or index, currency fluctuations,
volatilities, the financial condition of the
issuer and its creditworthiness.
− An ELN does not give you ownership rights
in the underlying share, or the shares
comprising the underlying index, and does
not entitle you to any voting rights or
dividend payments in respect of the shares
during the tenor of the ELN
− If the underlying is an index (or indices), the
index sponsors are responsible for
calculating and maintaining the underlying
index or indices. Actions of the index
sponsor, including changes to the
calculation methodology, may change the
value of the underlying index at any time.
The calculation of the repayment on the
redemption date will be adjusted to reflect
such event(s).
− You bear the risk that the issuer and the
guarantor (if any) of the ELN may become
insolvent, which may lead to a partial or
total loss of the principal amount.

Additional information – adjusting
the risk/ return profile
The risk/return profile of an ELN can be varied
by adjusting its terms. One of the most
common parameters that can be adjusted is
the strike level. The closer the strike level is to
the market price of the underlying share (or
index) on the trade date, the greater the risk
that the underlying share (or index) will close
below the strike level on the final fixing date,
which may result in a loss for you. The higher
risk is in return for a higher yield on the ELN.
The lower the strike level, the more defensive
the ELN would be, and consequently the
lower the yield on the ELN.
The secondary market price of an ELN
depends directly on the underlying share’s (or
index’s) performance. The ELN loses value
quickly when the price of the underlying
share declines, even if the underlying share is
still trading above the strike level. For this
reason, an ELN should not be seen as
comparable to traditional money market
investments.
The choice of underlying share (or index) is
the most influential factor in determining the
yield of an ELN. The more volatile the
underlying share (or index) is, and therefore
the more likely that the underlying may fall
below the strike level, the higher the yield.
The currency of the investment is also a
factor, as interest rates vary from currency to
currency. However, this does not mean that
an ELN has to be structured in the same
currency as the underlying share (or index).
Additional information – Price
behavior of a “Worst of” ELN
The secondary market price of a “Worst of”
ELN is affected in a similar way to that of an
ELN. It should be remembered that the price is,
above all, influenced by the price movement of
the worst performing share (or index). In
addition, price behavior is also affected by the
correlation between the underlying shares (or
indices). If the correlation between the
underlying shares (or indices) increases during
the tenor, this could have a small positive
impact on the price of the “Worst of” ELN.
Correlation should also be taken into account
when structuring the terms of a “Worst of” ELN
at the beginning of the tenor. The lower the
correlation between the underlying shares (or
indices), the more attractive the terms, or the
higher the yield, of the “Worst of” ELN. On the
other hand, if two companies are in the same
industry, their share (or index) prices are
expected to have similar movements or higher
correlation. In this case it is less likely that only
one share (or index) will fall below the strike
level. You will receive a lower yield in return for
this lower risk on a “Worst of” ELN.
Additional information – Rounding
The number of shares for physical delivery is
calculated by dividing the denomination of the
ELN by the strike level of the underlying share.
The actual number of shares for delivery will be
the nearest whole number to this figure, while
you will receive on the redemption date a cash
equivalent for the fractional share, calculated by
reference to the closing price of the underlying
share on final fixing date.
