Unit 1: Multiplication (2-digit and 3-digit numbers × 2-digit numbers)

In this unit you will learn to:
- multiply a 2-digit number by a 2-digit number in column form
- multiply a 3-digit number by a 2-digit number, with carrying
- use quick tricks to multiply in an easier order
- estimate to check if your answer makes sense

Let's start
The school hall has 24 rows of chairs. There are 18 chairs in each row. How many chairs are there? We need 24 × 18. In P3 you multiplied by a 1-digit number. Now we multiply by a 2-digit number.

Multiplying by a 2-digit number: split the tens and the ones
24 × 18 means 24 × 8 (the ones) plus 24 × 10 (the tens).
- Step 1: 24 × 8 = 192. Write 192.
- Step 2: 24 × 10 = 240. Write 240 under it. (Many children write a 0 in the ones place first, then work out 24 × 1 = 24 next to it.)
- Step 3: Add: 192 + 240 = 432.
So 24 × 18 = 432. There are 432 chairs.

Example 1: 36 × 27
- 36 × 7 = 252 (6 × 7 = 42, write 2, carry 4; 3 × 7 = 21, add the 4 = 25)
- 36 × 20 = 720
- 252 + 720 = 972
So 36 × 27 = 972.

Multiplying a 3-digit number by a 2-digit number
Example 2: 215 × 34
- 215 × 4 = 860
- 215 × 30 = 6450
- 860 + 6450 = 7310
So 215 × 34 = 7310.

Example 3: 408 × 25 (a 0 in the middle)
- 408 × 5 = 2040
- 408 × 20 = 8160
- 2040 + 8160 = 10 200
So 408 × 25 = 10 200.

Remember the carrying! Write small carried digits clearly and add them after you multiply, not before.

Quick tricks: change the order
You can multiply numbers in any order and group them in any way. The answer stays the same.
- 25 × 53 × 4 = 53 × (25 × 4) = 53 × 100 = 5300
- 5 × 37 × 2 = 37 × (5 × 2) = 37 × 10 = 370
- 125 × 8 = 1000, so 125 × 16 × 8 = 16 × 1000 = 16 000
Look for pairs that make 10, 100 or 1000: 2 × 5, 4 × 25, 8 × 125, 20 × 50.

Estimate to check
Round each number, then multiply.
- 48 × 31 is about 50 × 30 = 1500. The exact answer is 1488, which is close, so it makes sense.
- If you get 14 880 or 148, you know something went wrong.

Word problems
Read the question, write a number sentence, work it out, then write an answer sentence.
Example 4: A box holds 144 pencils. How many pencils are there in 15 boxes?
144 × 15 = 2160
Answer: There are 2160 pencils.

Common mistakes
- Forgetting the 0 when you multiply by the tens: 24 × 18 is NOT 192 + 24.
- Adding the carried digit before multiplying: in 36 × 7, do 3 × 7 = 21 first, then add the carried 4.
- Lining up the digits in the wrong columns when adding the two rows.

Challenge (for fast learners)
- 2-digit × 3-digit works the same way: 32 × 125 = 125 × 32 = 4000.
- Find the missing digit: 4□ × 12 = 504. (Hint: 504 ÷ 12 = 42.)

Key words
- multiply: find the total of equal groups (乘)
- product: the answer to a multiplication (積)
- column form: working with the numbers written in columns (直式)
- carry: move a ten to the next column (進位)
- tens: the second place from the right (十位)
- estimate: find an answer that is close, to check your work (估算)
- number sentence: a line of working such as 24 × 18 = 432 (算式)
