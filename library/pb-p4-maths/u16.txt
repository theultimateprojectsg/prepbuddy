Unit 17: Area

In this unit you will learn to:
- understand what area is
- compare areas by looking, by overlapping and by counting squares
- use square centimetres (cm²) and square metres (m²)
- use formulas for the areas of squares and rectangles
- find the area of shapes made of squares and rectangles

Let's start
Which is bigger, the top of your desk or your exercise book? The amount of flat surface a shape covers is its area.

Comparing areas
- By looking: a football pitch is clearly bigger than a classroom floor.
- By overlapping: put one shape on top of the other. If it covers the other completely, it has the bigger area.
- With a third shape: if A is bigger than B and B is bigger than C, then A is bigger than C.
- By counting units: count how many stamps, tiles or squares cover each shape. Use the same unit for both!

Square centimetres and square metres
- 1 square centimetre (1 cm²) is the area of a square with sides of 1 cm. Good for small things: a stamp, a book cover.
- 1 square metre (1 m²) is the area of a square with sides of 1 m. Good for big things: a classroom floor, a wall.
- On squared paper with 1 cm squares, count the squares to find the area in cm². Two half squares make one whole square.

Area of a rectangle
A rectangle 5 cm long and 3 cm wide can be covered by 3 rows of 5 squares: 15 squares.
Area of a rectangle = length × width
Example 1: 5 × 3 = 15. The area is 15 cm².

Area of a square
Area of a square = side × side
Example 2: a square floor tile with side 30 cm: 30 × 30 = 900. The area is 900 cm².

Working backwards
- A rectangle has an area of 48 m² and a length of 8 m. Width = 48 ÷ 8 = 6 m.
- A square has an area of 49 cm². Each side is 7 cm (because 7 × 7 = 49).

Shapes made of rectangles and squares
Split the shape into rectangles, find each area, then add.
Example 3: An L-shape is made of a rectangle 10 cm × 4 cm and a rectangle 5 cm × 3 cm.
Area = 10 × 4 + 5 × 3 = 40 + 15 = 55 cm².
Or take a piece away: a 10 m × 8 m garden has a 3 m × 2 m pond. The grass area is 10 × 8 − 3 × 2 = 80 − 6 = 74 m².

Area or perimeter?
- Area: how much surface is covered (painting a wall, laying a carpet). Units: cm², m².
- Perimeter: how far it is round the edge (a fence, a frame, a ribbon border). Units: cm, m.
Example 4: A 6 m × 4 m room. Carpet needed (area): 6 × 4 = 24 m². Skirting board needed (perimeter): (6 + 4) × 2 = 20 m.

Common mistakes
- Using cm instead of cm² for area.
- Adding the sides (perimeter) when the question asks for area.
- Counting squares of different sizes as if they were the same unit.
- You do NOT need to change between cm² and m² in P4.

Challenge (for fast learners)
- A rectangle has an area of 36 cm². Its length and width are whole numbers. List all the possible rectangles. (1 × 36, 2 × 18, 3 × 12, 4 × 9, 6 × 6)
- Which of these rectangles has the biggest perimeter? (1 × 36: perimeter 74 cm)

Key words
- area: the amount of surface a shape covers (面積)
- square centimetre (cm²): the area of a 1 cm by 1 cm square (平方厘米)
- square metre (m²): the area of a 1 m by 1 m square (平方米)
- overlap: put one on top of the other (重疊)
- squared paper: paper with small squares (方格紙)
- cover: spread over the whole surface (覆蓋)
- unit: the size you count with (單位)
