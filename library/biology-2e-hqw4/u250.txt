The characteristic features of the Chordata are a
notochord, a dorsal hollow nerve cord, pharyngeal slits,
a post-anal tail, and an endostyle/thyroid that secretes
iodinated hormones. The phylum Chordata contains
two clades of invertebrates: Urochordata (tunicates,
salps, and larvaceans) and Cephalochordata
(lancelets), together with the vertebrates in the
Vertebrata. Most tunicates live on the ocean floor and
are suspension feeders. Lancelets are suspension
feeders that feed on phytoplankton and other
microorganisms. The sister taxon of the Chordates is
the Ambulacraria, which includes both the
Echinoderms and the hemichordates, which share
pharyngeal slits with the chordates.
