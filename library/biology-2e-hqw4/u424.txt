In the 1980s, biologists working in Lake Victoria in Africa discovered one of the most
extraordinary products of evolution on the planet. Located in the Great Rift Valley, Lake Victoria is an enormous and
deep lake about 68,900 km2 in area (larger than Lake Huron, the second largest of North America’s Great Lakes).
Biologists were studying species of a family of fish called cichlids. When they sampled for fish in different locations
of the lake, the researchers identified over 500 evolved species in total. However, the scientists soon discovered
that the invasive Nile Perch was destroying the lake’s cichlid population, bringing hundreds of cichlid species to
extinction with devastating rapidity.
