Unit 5: How much? How many? (countable and uncountable nouns)

In this unit you will learn to:
- tell countable and uncountable nouns apart
- use some, any, much, many, a few, a little
- use enough, too many, too much, more, fewer and less

Read first: Making fried rice
Ben and his dad are making fried rice for dinner. "How much rice do we need?" asks Ben. "Two bowls of rice," says Dad. Ben opens the fridge. There are some eggs, a few carrots and a little ham, but there aren't any onions. "How many eggs should I use?" "Three, please," says Dad. Ben cracks the eggs into a bowl. Dad adds a little oil to the pan and cooks the eggs first. Then he puts in the rice, the carrots and the ham. "Do we need any salt?" asks Ben. "Just a little," says Dad. Soon the kitchen is full of a lovely smell. Ben's sister comes in with a few glasses of water. "Dinner is ready!" says Ben proudly.
As you read, look for how much / how many, some / any, a few / a little, and things you can and can't count.

Countable nouns are things we can count: one apple, two apples, a pencil, three pencils, a child, four children.
- Plurals: add -s (books), -es after s, sh, ch, x, o (buses, dishes, watches, boxes, tomatoes), consonant + y → -ies (stories, cherries), f/fe → -ves (leaf → leaves, knife → knives).
- Irregular plurals: man → men, woman → women, child → children, foot → feet, tooth → teeth, mouse → mice, sheep → sheep, fish → fish.

Uncountable nouns are things we can't count one by one: water, milk, rice, bread, sugar, money, homework, information, furniture, music, paper.
- They have no plural: NOT "breads", "homeworks", "furnitures".
- Use a unit to count them: a glass of milk, a bowl of rice, a piece of paper, a slice of bread, a bottle of water, a cup of tea, a bag of sugar, two loaves of bread.

Some and any
- some in positive sentences: There are some eggs in the fridge. I want some juice.
- any in negatives and most questions: There aren't any eggs. Is there any juice?
- some in offers and requests: Would you like some cake? Can I have some water, please?

Much, many, a lot of, a few, a little
- many + countable plurals: How many students are there? There aren't many chairs.
- much + uncountable: How much milk do we need? I don't have much time.
- a lot of / lots of + both: We have a lot of books. There is a lot of noise.
- a few + countable (a small number): I have a few friends in Class 4B.
- a little + uncountable (a small amount): Add a little salt.
- plenty of + both (more than enough): Don't hurry, we have plenty of time. There are plenty of seats.

Enough, too many, too much
- enough = the right amount: Have we got enough chairs? There isn't enough milk for everyone.
- too many + countable plurals (more than we want): There are too many people on the bus.
- too much + uncountable: Don't put too much sugar in your tea.

More, fewer, less
- more + both: We need more cups. Can I have more rice?
- fewer + countable plurals: Ann made fewer mistakes than Joe.
- less + uncountable: Please use less water. I have less homework today.

There is / There are
- There is + singular or uncountable: There is a cat on the sofa. There is some rice in the pot.
- There are + plural: There are some oranges on the table.

Common mistakes
- "How many water do you drink?" → How much water do you drink?
- "I have many homeworks." → I have a lot of homework.
- "There is some apples." → There are some apples.
- "There are too much cars." → There are too many cars.
- "I have less books than you." → I have fewer books than you.

Key words
- fried rice: rice cooked in a pan with oil (炒飯)
- fridge: a cold box that keeps food fresh (雪櫃)
- carrot: an orange vegetable (紅蘿蔔)
- onion: a round vegetable that can make you cry (洋蔥)
- crack: break an egg open (打開蛋)
- oil: a liquid for cooking (油)
- proudly: feeling happy about what you did (自豪地)

Your task
Write a shopping list and three sentences about making your favourite snack (about 50 words). Use some, any, a few, a little, how much and how many.
