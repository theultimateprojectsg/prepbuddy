Unit 13: Word power (adverbs, prefixes and word partners)

In this unit you will learn to:
- describe how people do things (quickly, carefully, well)
- make opposites with un-, im-, in- and dis-
- use word partners like good at and afraid of
- find and correct mistakes (proofreading)

Adverbs of manner tell us how someone does something.
- Most are made from adjectives + -ly: quick → quickly, slow → slowly, careful → carefully, loud → loudly, quiet → quietly, sad → sadly, sudden → suddenly
- Adjectives ending in -y: change y to i: happy → happily, easy → easily, angry → angrily, noisy → noisily
- Irregular: good → well, fast → fast, hard → hard, early → early, late → late
- Adjective or adverb? An adjective describes a noun: She is a careful writer. An adverb describes a verb: She writes carefully. He is a good singer. He sings well. (NOT "He sings good.")
- Adverbs of manner usually come after the verb (and after the object): The children walked quietly. Please close the door gently.

Prefixes change the meaning of a word. Many make the opposite.
- un-: happy → unhappy, kind → unkind, lucky → unlucky, tidy → untidy, comfortable → uncomfortable, lock → unlock
- im-: polite → impolite, possible → impossible, patient → impatient
- in-: correct → incorrect, expensive → inexpensive, convenient → inconvenient
- dis-: like → dislike, agree → disagree, appear → disappear, honest → dishonest
- re- (again): write → rewrite, read → reread, use → reuse, fill → refill
- non- (not): non-stop, non-smoking

Word partners: adjective + preposition (learn them as one phrase)
- good at: Mei is good at drawing.
- bad at: I am bad at singing.
- afraid of: My brother is afraid of the dark.
- interested in: Are you interested in science?
- proud of: Mum is proud of me.
- kind to / nice to / mean to: Be kind to animals.
- angry with (a person): The teacher was angry with the noisy boys.
- different from: My hobby is different from my sister's.
- full of: The box is full of books.
- made of: This table is made of wood.
- After a preposition, use a noun or verb-ing: He is good at swimming. (NOT "good at swim")

Proofreading: finding and fixing mistakes
When you check a piece of writing, look for:
1. Tense: Yesterday I go (went) to the park.
2. Verb agreement: She like (likes) apples.
3. Plurals: three box (boxes)
4. Spelling: freind (friend), becuase (because)
5. Capital letters and full stops: on monday (Monday)
6. Pronouns: Him (He) is my friend.
7. Word partners: I am good in (at) maths.

Proofreading practice: there is one mistake in each line.
Last sunday, my family went to the beach.
My little sister was afraid at the waves.
I swimmed in the sea with my dad.
We ate sandwichs under a big umbrella.
My mum sang happy while she was cooking the sausages.
(Answers: Sunday; afraid of; swam; sandwiches; happily)

Key words
- carefully: with care (小心地)
- quickly: fast (快速地)
- impolite: not polite (無禮的)
- untidy: not tidy, messy (不整齊的)
- be good at: can do something well (擅長)
- be afraid of: feel scared of (害怕)
- proofread: check writing for mistakes (校對)

Your task
Write five sentences about your classmates or family (about 50 words). Use two adverbs (e.g. carefully, quietly), one word with a prefix (un-, im-, dis-) and two word partners (e.g. good at, kind to). Then check your writing for mistakes.
