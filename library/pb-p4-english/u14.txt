Unit 15: Rules, advice and suggestions (must, should, let's, would like)

Read first: Planning a class camp
Class 4C is going on a camp next month, and Miss Ho is talking to the class. "You must bring a sleeping bag and a torch," she says. "You mustn't bring any snacks, because there are animals near the camp. You don't have to bring a towel. The camp has towels for everyone." Ricky puts up his hand. "Should we bring a raincoat?" "Yes, you should. It often rains in the mountains," says Miss Ho. After class, Ricky and his friends make a plan. "Let's buy a new torch together," says Ricky. "Shall we go to the shop on Saturday?" asks Amy. "Why don't we go on Sunday instead? I have a piano lesson on Saturday," says Ben. "OK. How about meeting at the MTR station at ten?" says Ricky. Everyone agrees. "Which torch would you like, the small one or the big one?" asks Amy. "I'd like the small one. It's lighter," says Ben. "You ought to buy some batteries too," says Ricky.
As you read, look for must / mustn't, have to / don't have to, should / ought to, let's, shall we, how about and would like.

must / mustn't + base verb: rules and things that are very important
- You must wear your seat belt. Students must be on time.
- mustn't (must not) = it is not allowed: You mustn't run in the corridor. We mustn't eat in the library.
- have to / has to also means it is necessary: I have to finish my homework tonight. She has to wear glasses.
- don't have to = it is not necessary (but you can): It's Saturday, so I don't have to get up early.

should / shouldn't and ought to: advice (a good idea)
- You should drink more water. You shouldn't watch TV so late.
- ought to means the same as should: You ought to say sorry to her. We ought to bring an umbrella.
- Asking for advice: What should I do? Should I tell the teacher?

Suggestions: let's, shall we, why don't we, how about
- Let's + base verb: Let's play football after school. Let's not be late.
- Shall we + base verb?: Shall we go to the beach on Sunday? Yes, let's! / Good idea!
- Why don't we + base verb?: Why don't we make a card for Miss Chan?
- How about + verb-ing / noun?: How about going to the library? How about pizza?
- Offers: Shall I carry your bag? Shall I open the window?

want to and would like: wanting things
- want / want to: I want a new bike. He wants to be a pilot.
- would like is more polite: I'd like (I would like) a cup of tea, please. She would like to visit Japan.
- Offering: Would you like some juice? Yes, please. / No, thank you.
- Choosing: Which would you like, the red one or the blue one? I'd like the blue one, please. Would you like tea or coffee?

Common mistakes
- "You must to wear a helmet." → You must wear a helmet.
- "He should goes to bed." → He should go to bed.
- "Let's to go!" → Let's go!
- "I would like go swimming." → I would like to go swimming.
- "How about go to the park?" → How about going to the park?
- "You mustn't come tomorrow" (when you mean it's not necessary) → You don't have to come tomorrow.

Key words
- camp: a trip where you stay outdoors (營)
- sleeping bag: a warm bag you sleep in (睡袋)
- torch: a small light you carry (電筒)
- snack: a small amount of food between meals (小食)
- raincoat: a coat that keeps you dry (雨衣)
- agree: think the same (同意)
- batteries: things that give power to a torch or toy (電池)

Your task
Write the rules and plans for a class picnic (about 50 words). Give two rules with must or mustn't, one piece of advice with should, and one suggestion with Let's or Shall we.
