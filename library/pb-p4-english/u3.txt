Unit 4: Comparing things (comparatives and superlatives)

Read first: Choosing a pet
Mia wants a pet, so her family visits a pet shop. First, they look at a hamster. It is the smallest animal in the shop, and it is cheaper than the other pets. Then they look at a rabbit. The rabbit is bigger than the hamster, and its fur is softer. "Rabbits are quieter than dogs," says Mia's mum. Last, they see a puppy. It is the cutest animal in the shop, but it is also the most expensive. "A puppy needs more space than a rabbit," says Dad. "Our flat isn't as big as Grandma's house." In the end, Mia chooses the rabbit. She thinks it is the best pet for their small flat. She calls it Snowball because it is as white as snow.
As you read, look for comparing words: cheaper, bigger, the cutest, the most expensive, as big as.

We compare two things with comparatives, and three or more with superlatives.

Short adjectives (one syllable): add -er / -est
- tall → taller → the tallest; fast → faster → the fastest; cheap → cheaper → the cheapest
- Ending in -e: add -r / -st: nice → nicer → the nicest; large → larger → the largest
- One vowel + one consonant: double the last letter: big → bigger → the biggest; hot → hotter → the hottest; thin → thinner → the thinnest
- Ending in consonant + y: change y to i: happy → happier → the happiest; easy → easier → the easiest; heavy → heavier → the heaviest

Long adjectives (most adjectives with two or more syllables): more / the most
- beautiful → more beautiful → the most beautiful
- expensive → more expensive → the most expensive
- interesting → more interesting → the most interesting

Irregular adjectives
- good → better → the best
- bad → worse → the worst
- far → farther (or further) → the farthest (or furthest)
- many / much → more → the most

How to use them
- Comparative + than: My bag is heavier than your bag. Science is more interesting than History for me.
- The + superlative: Tai Mo Shan is the highest mountain in Hong Kong. Ken is the fastest runner in our class.
- Same: as + adjective + as: Peter is as tall as his father. This book isn't as long as that one.
- The same as / different from: My school bag is the same as yours. Your answer is different from mine. The twins look the same, but they like different food.
- (not) the same + noun + as: Tom is the same age as Ben. My pencil case isn't the same colour as hers.

Common mistakes
- "She is more taller than me." → She is taller than me.
- "This is the most big cake." → This is the biggest cake.
- "My pencil is gooder." → My pencil is better.
- "He is tall than me." → He is taller than me.
- "My bag is same as yours." → My bag is the same as yours.
- "Your idea is different with mine." → Your idea is different from mine.

Key words
- hamster: a small furry pet (倉鼠)
- rabbit: a pet with long ears (兔子)
- puppy: a young dog (小狗)
- fur: the soft hair on an animal (毛)
- expensive: costs a lot of money (昂貴的)
- space: room to move (空間)
- choose: pick one (選擇)

Your task
Compare two or three animals or toys you know (about 50 words). Use at least two comparatives (-er / more), one superlative (the -est / the most) and one sentence with as … as.
