Unit 12: Can and could (ability, permission and requests)

Read first: Then and now
When Grandpa was a boy, life in Hong Kong was very different. "We couldn't watch TV at home because we didn't have one," he says. "We could only listen to the radio." Grandpa's family couldn't afford many toys, so he made kites from paper and bamboo. He could fly his kites very high on the hills near his village. He couldn't speak English then, but he learnt it later at night school. Now Grandpa can speak English quite well, and he can even use a smartphone. "Could you teach me how to make a kite?" asks his granddaughter, Lucy. "Of course I can," says Grandpa with a big smile.
As you read, look for can, can't, could and couldn't for ability now and in the past, and Could you …? to ask politely.

can + base verb: ability now, permission and requests
- Ability: I can swim. My grandma can speak three languages. Penguins can't fly.
- Permission: You can use my eraser. Can I go out to play? Yes, you can. / No, you can't.
- Requests: Can you pass me the salt, please?
- can is the same for everyone: he can, she can, they can (NOT "he cans"). After can, use the base verb: She can ride a bike (NOT "can rides" or "can to ride").

could + base verb: ability in the past, and polite requests
- Past ability: When I was three, I could count to ten, but I couldn't read. Last year Ken couldn't swim, but now he can.
- Polite requests: Could you open the window, please? Could I borrow your pen, please?
- Negative: could not = couldn't. We couldn't see the stage because a tall man was in front of us.
- Questions: Could you ride a bike when you were five? Yes, I could. / No, I couldn't.

Can or could?
- Now or in general → can: I can play the piano now.
- In the past → could: I couldn't play the piano two years ago.
- Asking politely → could (more polite than can): Could you help me, please?

Common mistakes
- "I can to swim." → I can swim.
- "Yesterday I can't find my book." → Yesterday I couldn't find my book.
- "She cans dance." → She can dance.

Key words
- afford: have enough money to buy (負擔得起)
- kite: a toy that flies in the wind on a string (風箏)
- bamboo: a tall plant with hard stems (竹)
- village: a small place in the countryside (鄉村)
- night school: classes for adults in the evening (夜校)
- smartphone: a phone that can use apps (智能電話)
- granddaughter: the daughter of your son or daughter (孫女)

Your task
Write about you now and when you were five (about 50 words). Use could and couldn't for when you were five, can and can't for now, and one polite question with Could you …?
