Unit 7: Whose is it? (pronouns and possessives)

Read first: The lost-and-found box
After the sports day, Miss Chan brought a big box into Class 4A. "These things are in the lost-and-found box," she said. "Whose are they?" She held up a red water bottle. "It's mine!" said Jenny. "My mum bought it for me last week." Next, Miss Chan held up a blue cap. "Is it yours, Ricky?" "No, it isn't mine. I think it's Kevin's. His cap is blue." Kevin smiled and took it. "Thank you! I looked for it myself for a long time." At the bottom of the box, there were two jackets. "They're ours," said the twins, Ivy and Iris. "We left them on the bench." Miss Chan laughed. "Please write your names on your things next time!"
As you read, look for whose, mine / yours / ours, Kevin's, and myself.

Subject pronouns do the action: I, you, he, she, it, we, they
- Tom is my friend. He lives in Tsuen Wan.
Object pronouns receive the action or come after a preposition: me, you, him, her, it, us, them
- Please give the ball to me. I like Amy. I play with her every day.

Possessive adjectives come before a noun: my, your, his, her, its, our, their
- This is my pencil case. They love their dog. The dog wagged its tail. (its = belonging to it; it's = it is)

Possessive pronouns stand alone (no noun after them): mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs
- "Is this your umbrella?" "Yes, it's mine." That umbrella is hers. These seats are ours.

Table
I – me – my – mine
you – you – your – yours
he – him – his – his
she – her – her – hers
it – it – its – (not used)
we – us – our – ours
they – them – their – theirs

Apostrophe s ('s) for people and animals
- one owner: Ka Ming's bike, my sister's room, the cat's bowl
- plural owners ending in -s: add only ': the students' bags, my parents' car
- irregular plurals: add 's: the children's toys, the women's team

Asking about owners: Whose …?
- Whose bag is this? It's Peter's. / It's his.
- Whose shoes are these? They're mine.

Reflexive pronouns (when the subject and object are the same person): myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves
- I made this card myself. The children enjoyed themselves at the party.
- by + reflexive pronoun = alone, without help: My little brother can tie his shoes by himself now. I walked to school by myself.

Everybody, somebody, nobody, anybody (and everyone, someone, no one, anyone)
- They take a singular verb: Everybody is here. Nobody knows the answer. Somebody is knocking at the door.
- everybody / everyone = all the people; nobody / no one = not one person; somebody / someone = one person (we don't know who).
- anybody / anyone in questions and negatives: Is anybody at home? I didn't see anyone.
- Don't use "not" with nobody: "Nobody didn't come." → Nobody came.
- Things: everything, something, nothing, anything: There is nothing in the box.

Common mistakes
- "Me and my friend went shopping." → My friend and I went shopping.
- "This is mine book." → This is my book. / This book is mine.
- "The dog hurt it's leg." → The dog hurt its leg.
- "Is this your's?" → Is this yours?

Key words
- lost-and-found: a place for things people have lost (失物認領)
- water bottle: a bottle you drink water from (水樽)
- cap: a hat with a peak at the front (鴨舌帽)
- jacket: a short coat (外套)
- bench: a long seat (長凳)
- twins: two children born at the same time (雙胞胎)
- bottom: the lowest part (底部)

Your task
Write a short conversation (about 6 lines) about finding things in a classroom. Use Whose …?, mine, yours, his, hers, ours and one 's (e.g. Kevin's).
