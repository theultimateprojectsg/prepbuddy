Unit 9: Asking questions (question words and question forms)

Question words (wh- words)
- What: things and actions: What is your favourite food? What are you doing?
- Who: people: Who is your class teacher? Who broke the window?
- Where: places: Where do you live? Where did you put my keys?
- When: time: When is your birthday? When does the film start?
- Why: reasons: Why are you late? (Answer with "Because…")
- How: the way: How do you go to school? I go by MTR.
- Which: a choice from a group: Which colour do you like, red or blue?
- Whose: the owner: Whose pencil is this?
- How many / How much: number and amount: How many brothers do you have? How much is this T-shirt? It's $80.
- How old / How long / How often / How far: How often do you swim? Twice a week. How long is the film? Two hours.

Making questions
1. With be (am / is / are / was / were): move the verb before the subject.
   She is happy. → Is she happy? They were at home. → Where were they?
2. With can / will / should: move it before the subject.
   He can swim. → Can he swim? You will come. → When will you come?
3. With other verbs: use do / does / did + subject + base verb.
   You like pizza. → Do you like pizza?
   She plays tennis. → Does she play tennis? (NOT "Does she plays")
   They went to Macau. → Where did they go? (NOT "Where did they went")

Questions about the subject (who / what did the action) don't need do/does/did:
- Who called you? Mary called me. What happened? A cup fell down.

Short answers
- Are you hungry? Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.
- Does he like cats? Yes, he does. / No, he doesn't.
- Can you ride a bike? Yes, I can. / No, I can't.

Polite questions
- Can I borrow your ruler, please? Could you help me, please? May I go to the toilet, please?

Common mistakes
- "Where you live?" → Where do you live?
- "What time the bus comes?" → What time does the bus come?
- "Why you are sad?" → Why are you sad?

Practice passage: An interview with the school caretaker
Our class newspaper team interviewed Mr Lau, the school caretaker.
Reporter: When did you start working at our school, Mr Lau?
Mr Lau: Twelve years ago.
Reporter: What time do you start work every day?
Mr Lau: I start at half past six. I open the gates and turn on the lights.
Reporter: What is the hardest part of your job?
Mr Lau: Fixing things on rainy days. Sometimes water comes into the hall.
Reporter: Which part of the school do you like best?
Mr Lau: The garden. I planted the sunflowers myself.
Reporter: Why do you like your job?
Mr Lau: Because I can see the students every day, and they always say good morning to me.
